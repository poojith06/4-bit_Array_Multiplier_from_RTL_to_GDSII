on SkyWater SKY130 with Open-Source Tools}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We designed a 4-bit $\times$ 4-bit unsigned array multiplier in Verilog
with registered inputs and outputs (16 flip-flops, 2-cycle latency),
verified it exhaustively, synthesised it to the SKY130
\cell{sky130_fd_sc_hd} library with Yosys, analysed its timing with
OpenSTA and took it to a GDSII layout with LibreLane. RTL and gate-level
simulation both pass all 256 input combinations. Synthesis gives 67 cells
(784.502\,$\mu$m$^2$) and STA gives $T_{\min}=2.980$\,ns
($F_{\max}=335.57$\,MHz) at the typical corner. LibreLane needed 7 runs
and closed timing at a 7.0\,ns clock across all its default corners, with
0 DRC and 0 LVS errors. Post-route $F_{\max}$ is 144.70\,MHz at the worst
(slow) corner and 288.04\,MHz at the typical corner.
\end{abstract}

\section{Part A --- RTL Design and Verification}

\subsection{Design}
\cell{rtl/full_adder.v} is a 1-bit full adder written only with
\texttt{assign} statements: $\mathit{sum}=a\oplus b\oplus c_{in}$ and
$c_{out}=ab+(a\oplus b)\,c_{in}$. The carry form shares the $a\oplus b$
term with the sum; it is logically identical to the majority form
$ab+bc_{in}+ac_{in}$.
\cell{rtl/mult_array.v} registers \texttt{a} and \texttt{b} into
\cell{a_reg}/\cell{b_reg} (8 flip-flops), forms the 16 partial-product
bits $pp_i[j]=a[j]\wedge b[i]$, and adds them with three rows of four
\cell{full_adder} instances (\cell{fa_r1_0} \dots\ \cell{fa_r3_3}, 12 in
total) wired exactly as in the connection table of Section~2 of the
assignment. The 8-bit sum is registered into \texttt{p} (8 flip-flops).
All 16 flip-flops are cleared by the asynchronous active-low reset
\cell{rst_n}. No \texttt{*} operator is used in the design.
Fig.~\ref{fig:block} shows the block diagram.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering

\caption{Block diagram of \cell{mult_array}: input registers, the 16 AND

\subsection{Testbench}
\cell{tb/tb_mult.v} generates a 10\,ns clock, holds \cell{rst_n} low for
the first two cycles and applies all 256 combinations of $a$ and $b$. Each
input is applied on a falling edge; the testbench waits two rising edges
(the 2-cycle latency) and compares \texttt{p} with \texttt{a*b} at the
next falling edge. It prints a line for every mismatch, one summary line
at the end, and dumps \cell{mult.vcd}.

\subsection{Hand-worked example}
Sai Poojith's roll number 2025122010 ends in 0, so
$A = 0 + 5 = 5$ and $B = 7$.
\begin{lstlisting}[columns=fixed,keepspaces=true,breaklines=false]
        0 1 0 1  A = 5
      x 0 1 1 1  B = 7
---------------
        0 1 0 1  pp0 = A AND b0 (b0=1)
      0 1 0 1    pp1 = A AND b1 (b1=1), shifted 1
    0 1 0 1      pp2 = A AND b2 (b2=1), shifted 2

Fig.~\ref{fig:wave} shows the same multiplication in GTKWave. The inputs

\section{Part D --- Physical Design}

\cell{pnr/config.json} contains exactly the six settings from the
assignment: \cell{DESIGN_NAME}, \cell{VERILOG_FILES}, \cell{CLOCK_PORT},
\cell{CLOCK_PERIOD}, \cell{FP_SIZING} = \texttt{"absolute"} and
\cell{DIE_AREA} = \texttt{[0, 0, 150, 150]}. The flow was run with
\texttt{librelane config.json} (LibreLane v3.1.0.dev3) and its default
timing corners: typical, slow and fast process corners
(\cell{tt_025C_1v80}, \cell{ss_100C_1v60}, \cell{ff_n40C_1v95}), each with
nominal, minimum and maximum wire parasitics.

\subsection{Timing closure}
The starting period was
$1.2\times T_{\min} = 1.2\times 2.980 = 3.576$\,ns, rounded up to
4.0\,ns. Every failing run failed only on setup timing in the slow corner
\cell{ss_100C_1v60}, where cells are much slower than at the typical
corner used in Part~C. Following the assignment, we increased
\cell{CLOCK_PERIOD} by 0.5\,ns after each failure (Table~\ref{tab:runs}).
Synthesis was identical in all seven runs (68 cells,
765.734\,$\mu$m$^2$), and so was the layout up to clock-tree synthesis
(1252.45\,$\mu$m$^2$ of standard cells). The runs differ only in the
post-CTS timing-repair step (\cell{ResizerTimingPostCTS}), which upsizes
or buffers gates only where it predicts a setup violation from pre-route
wire estimates:
\begin{itemize}
\item from 4.0 to 5.5\,ns it added 197.69\,$\mu$m$^2$, and the slack
improved by about 0.5\,ns per run;
\item at 6.0\,ns it added 180.17\,$\mu$m$^2$;
\item at 6.5\,ns it added 23.77\,$\mu$m$^2$;
\item at 7.0\,ns it added 12.51\,$\mu$m$^2$.
\end{itemize}
With less repair, the slow-corner path got slower: data arrival was
5.97\,ns up to 5.5\,ns, then 6.15\,ns at 6.0\,ns, 6.66\,ns at 6.5\,ns and
6.91\,ns at 7.0\,ns. So the slack stopped improving at 6.0\,ns, got worse
from 6.0 to 6.5\,ns ($-0.1282\to-0.1568$\,ns), and became positive at
7.0\,ns ($+0.0890$\,ns).

\section{Answers to Questions}

\textbf{Q1. Logic cells.}
Synthesis produced $67 - 16 = 51$ logic cells, not $16 + 12 = 28$,
because the RTL's AND gates and full adders do not map one-to-one onto
library cells. The design was flattened, so ABC optimised all the logic
together. It dropped constant inputs (each row's carry-in and $r_0[3]$
are 0) and merged AND gates into adder logic (\cell{and4_1} computes
$a_3a_2b_0b_1$ directly). It also skipped the library's multi-output
\cell{fa_1} cell; its log says it ignored 9 multi-output cells. So each
full adder becomes several single-output cells, such as an XOR3 for the
sum plus a MAJ3 or AND-OR cells for the carry. How the RTL is written
also matters. With the earlier majority form
$c_{out}=ab+bc_{in}+ac_{in}$, the same flow gave 73 cells,
805.773\,$\mu$m$^2$ and one \cell{maj3}. The shared-$(a\oplus b)$ form
gives 67 cells, 784.502\,$\mu$m$^2$ and five \cell{maj3}, even though
both forms are logically identical. That version is smaller but slower
($T_{\min}$ 2.980\,ns against 2.596\,ns), because ABC's result depends on
the structure it starts from.

\textbf{Q2. Critical path.}
The critical path goes from input register \cell{b_reg[0]} (\cell{_106_})
to output register \cell{p_reg[6]} (\cell{_100_}, the sum of
\cell{fa_r3_3}). It has 10 cells: the launch flip-flop, 8 logic gates and
the capture flip-flop. The logic part takes $2.844 - 0.411 = 2.433$\,ns.
All 8 gates are full-adder carry and sum logic, rippling through the rows
to \cell{fa_r3_3}. The partial-product AND gates are merged into them
(\cell{and4_1} computes $pp_1[2]\,pp_0[3]$, the carry-generate of
\cell{fa_r1_2}). That matches the $2N = 8$ full-adder estimate. The
path ends with four direct full-adder cells (\cell{xnor3}, \cell{maj3},
\cell{maj3}, \cell{xor3}). The earlier gates (\cell{and4},
\cell{a2111oi}, \cell{a21oi}, \cell{mux2i}) are complex cells that
combine carry terms from several adders.

\textbf{Q3. Flip-flop area.}
The flip-flops take 400.384 of 784.502\,$\mu$m$^2$, which is 51.04\,\%.
Each \cell{dfrtp_1} is 25.024\,$\mu$m$^2$, because it contains two latches
plus asynchronous reset logic. Most logic gates in our netlist are only
3.75--10.01\,$\mu$m$^2$; just the 3 \cell{xnor3}/\cell{xor3} cells are
close to a flip-flop in size (22.5--23.8\,$\mu$m$^2$). The design is tiny (51 logic cells), so the 16
registers are a large share. For an $N$-bit multiplier the logic grows
like $N^2$ but there are only $4N$ flip-flops, so in a bigger multiplier
this percentage would drop.

\textbf{Q4. Why post-route $F_{\max}$ is lower, and the added cells.}
Two effects lower it. The first is \emph{multi-corner signoff}. Part~C
used only the typical corner, but LibreLane signs off on its slowest
corner, \cell{ss_100C_1v60}. There, on the critical path from
\cell{b_reg[1]} (\cell{_109_}) to \cell{p_reg[7]} (\cell{_103_},
D~=~\cell{fa_r3_3.cout}), data arrives at 6.915\,ns instead of 3.416\,ns
at tt in the same layout. That gives the worst-corner $F_{\max}$ of
144.70\,MHz. The second is \emph{the real layout}: even at tt, $F_{\max}$
falls from 335.57 to 288.04\,MHz. The tt effective period of 3.472\,ns
is the 3.097\,ns data path (launch clock to D) plus a 0.124\,ns setup
time, LibreLane's 0.25\,ns clock uncertainty and 0.0005\,ns of net clock
skew. The capture clock arrives 0.031\,ns earlier than the launch clock
(0.288 vs.\ 0.319\,ns), and a 0.030\,ns reconvergence credit almost
cancels that. The clock-tree latency itself cancels because it delays
both flip-flops. The data path is longer than Part~C's 2.844\,ns for
three reasons. Routed wires add RC, so the launch flip-flop's clk$\to$Q,
with a fanout of 8, is 0.508\,ns instead of 0.411\,ns. LibreLane's own
synthesis gives a different 68-cell netlist, whose critical path ends at
\cell{p[7]} instead of \cell{p[6]}. And at 7.0\,ns the post-CTS timing repair added
only 12.51\,$\mu$m$^2$. The flow also added cells: 245 tap cells tie the
n-wells and substrate to VPWR/VGND to prevent latch-up, and 1305 decap
cells are on-chip capacitors that steady the supply when many gates
switch. 260 fill cells close the gaps in the rows so wells, implants and
power rails stay continuous, and 3 \cell{clkbuf_16} cells form the clock
tree that reaches all 16 flip-flops with low skew. The remaining
buffers are 17 \cell{buf_2} on the input and output ports, 2 fanout
buffers that split \cell{rst_n} (16 reset pins exceed the maximum fanout
of 10), and 1 \cell{buf_4} inserted by the setup repair.

\textbf{Q5. DRC and LVS.}
DRC (design rule check) verifies that the layout geometry obeys the
foundry's manufacturing rules: minimum widths and spacings of each layer,
enclosures, via sizes, densities and so on. LVS (layout versus
schematic) extracts the transistors and connections from the layout and
checks that they match the netlist, so the layout is the circuit we
designed. Both must pass because they catch different failures. A
DRC-clean layout can still be the wrong circuit, and a layout that
matches the schematic can still be impossible to manufacture reliably
(shorts, opens, low yield). Our final layout has 0 DRC errors (Magic and
KLayout) and 0 LVS errors.

\textbf{Q6. Scaling to 8 bits.}
From Section~2, an $N$-bit array multiplier uses $N^2$ AND gates and
$N(N-1)$ full adders, and its longest path is about $2N$ full-adder
delays.
\begin{align*}
N=4:&\quad 4\cdot3 = 12\ \text{FAs},\quad 2\cdot4 = 8\ \text{FA delays}\\
N=8:&\quad 8\cdot7 = 56\ \text{FAs},\quad 2\cdot8 = 16\ \text{FA delays}
\end{align*}
So the number of full adders grows by $56/12 \approx 4.7\times$ (and the
AND gates from 16 to 64), but the critical path only doubles. Scaling
our Part~C logic delay: $T_{\min} \approx 0.411 + 2\times 2.433 + 0.136
\approx 5.41$\,ns, so $F_{\max}$ would drop from about 336\,MHz to about
185\,MHz at the typical corner. Here 0.411\,ns is the clk$\to$Q delay,
2.433\,ns is the logic delay and 0.136\,ns is the setup time. The area
grows roughly quadratically and the delay roughly linearly.
